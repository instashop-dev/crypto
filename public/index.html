Nothing here places or simulates a trade. Both thresholds (xchg_min_profit_pct, funding_min_annual_pct) are display flags: every priced row is persisted whatever they say, and raising one re-labels the rows already on disk rather than discarding anything. initial_usdt is immutable — it is the denominator of the historical P&L above.

Cross-exchange. The same market quoted on two venues: what it would cost to buy on the cheaper and sell on the dearer, in the same instant. Both books are read per scan (Binance over WebSocket, MEXC over REST) and they are not simultaneous — the WS snapshot accumulates over up to 4s while the REST reply is one instant — so timing skew, not depth or fees, is the main source of spreads that were never fillable. Treat a reported spread as an upper bound on an upper bound. Turn xchg_enabled off to leave the scan polling funding alone.

India mode. Both legs of a spread dispose of a VDA — USDT included — so both attract TDS. Two legs × 1% TDS ≈ 2% of notional against a spread edge measured in basis points, which is the arithmetic that ended the fill experiment: the withholding is on turnover, not on gains.

Cash-and-carry, observed only. Long spot, short the perp, collect funding — price risk cancels between the legs. No position is ever opened. Net annual % is the annualised rate less the round trip of fees — 2 spot legs at fee_rate plus 2 perp legs at perp_fee_rate, spread over funding_hold_days. The annualised figure assumes the next published rate repeats all year, which it will not; basis, margin and liquidation are not modelled. Every venue is polled on every board, so one symbol can appear more than once and the two full-board venues each contribute the 11 majors plus their own best 20 and worst 5 contracts — a deeply negative rate is a result, not noise.
