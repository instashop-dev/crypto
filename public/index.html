Nothing here places an order. Neither threshold (xchg_min_profit_pct, funding_min_annual_pct) is a write-time gate: every priced row is persisted whatever they say, and raising one re-labels the rows already on disk rather than discarding anything. funding_min_annual_pct does one thing more since Phase 15 — it is also the bar a board row must clear before a paper carry position is opened on it. initial_usdt is immutable, and no carry position ever moves it: it is the denominator of the historical P&L above.

Cross-exchange. The same market quoted on two venues: what it would cost to buy on the cheaper and sell on the dearer, in the same instant. Both books are read per scan (Binance over WebSocket, MEXC over REST) and they are not simultaneous — the WS snapshot accumulates over up to 4s while the REST reply is one instant — so timing skew, not depth or fees, is the main source of spreads that were never fillable. Treat a reported spread as an upper bound on an upper bound. Turn xchg_enabled off to leave the scan polling funding alone.

India mode. Both legs of a spread dispose of a VDA — USDT included — so both attract TDS. Two legs × 1% TDS ≈ 2% of notional against a spread edge measured in basis points, which is the arithmetic that ended the fill experiment: the withholding is on turnover, not on gains.

Skew is how far apart in time the two books were — the gap between the end of the Binance WebSocket window and the MEXC response. It is a lower bound: quotes inside the window can be seconds older still. Survived is the same trade, same direction, re-priced against the next scan’s books; “—” means not measured (too old to re-price, or waiting for the next scan). A spread that was real is still there a minute later; one the skew invented is not. Cash-and-carry. Long spot, short the perp, collect funding — price risk cancels between the legs. This board is the observation; the paper positions opened from it are in the panel below, and nothing is traded either way. Net annual % is the annualised rate less the round trip of fees — 2 spot legs at fee_rate plus 2 perp legs at perp_fee_rate, spread over funding_hold_days. The annualised figure assumes the next published rate repeats all year, which it will not; basis, margin and liquidation are not modelled. Every venue is polled on every board, so one symbol can appear more than once and the two full-board venues each contribute the 11 majors plus their own best 20 and worst 5 contracts — a deeply negative rate is a result, not noise.

All perps, no spot leg. Long the venue paying least and short the venue paying most on the same asset: delta-neutral, and the income is the difference between the two funding streams, which is often steadier than either rate. Each side is annualised on its own settlement cadence before the subtraction — venues do not share a clock. Net annual % is that differential less four perp legs at perp_fee_rate, spread over funding_hold_days.

Derived from the board above on every read, never stored, and never traded — the paper carry book below stays single-venue. A row marked unverified is a symbol outside the 11 majors: the same ticker can be two different projects on two venues, and the differential would then be between unrelated assets. Multiplier contracts (1000PEPE) are never matched against their unprefixed namesakes. Margin lives on two venues at once and is not modelled; neither is a one-sided liquidation, which is how this trade actually breaks.

Cash and carry to settlement. Buy the asset spot, sell a dated future on it, hold both to expiry. The future converges to spot at settlement by construction, so the return is locked in at entry — which is the one thing perp funding never offers. The funding board above annualises a rate that has to repeat about 1095 times to be worth what it says; a basis has no such assumption in it.

Net annual % is amortised per contract. The four legs (2 spot at fee_rate, 2 futures at perp_fee_rate) are spread over each contract’s own remaining life rather than over funding_hold_days, because this trade ends when the contract does. A fat near-dated basis therefore routinely ranks below a thin far-dated one, and the table is sorted by net for exactly that reason.

What is not modelled. Margin and liquidation, above all: the short future needs collateral for months, it earns nothing here, and a rally big enough to liquidate it before expiry turns a locked-in return into a realised loss. Both legs are marked at the mid of the book, so no bid/ask spread is charged; a contract marked * had at least one leg priced off its last trade instead, because a side of its book was empty — and a stale mark is exactly what floats a fake basis to the top of a table sorted by net. The futures leg is charged the perp taker rate, which is an approximation. Inverse (coin-margined) and XPERP contracts are excluded — the first is not delta-neutral against a USDT spot leg, the second is a perp wearing an expiry. Observation only: no paper position is ever opened on a basis.

Paper positions, and still nothing is traded. Long spot, short the perp, at funding_position_size_usdt a leg — recorded here and never booked against the balance above, because a position held for days cannot be reconciled against a P&L whose whole history is atomic round trips. Funding accrues only for settlements the scanner actually observed; a boundary with no rate row inside 24h is skipped rather than estimated, which is why “settled” can lag the position’s age. Nothing is ever opened on an assumed settlement interval.

Predicted % vs realised % is the point. The first is what the rate observed at entry said a year of it would pay; the second is what the position actually paid, net of one round trip of fees, annualised over the days it was really held. Their difference is the “this rate repeats ~1095 times” extrapolation error — measured here rather than asserted. Basis and mark-to-market are not modelled at all, so a realised figure is the funding result, not the trade’s.

Realised % annualises a short hold aggressively. It scales the days actually held up to a year — a position closed after three days is multiplied by about 122, so a few cents of funding either way becomes a double-digit percentage. Read it against “Held” and against “Realised” in USDT, which is the figure that does not move when the hold is short.
