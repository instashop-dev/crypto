Nothing here places an order. Neither threshold (xchg_min_profit_pct, funding_min_annual_pct) is a write-time gate: every priced row is persisted whatever they say, and raising one re-labels the rows already on disk rather than discarding anything. funding_min_annual_pct does one thing more since Phase 15 — it is also the bar a board row must clear before a paper carry position is opened on it. initial_usdt is immutable, and no carry position ever moves it: it is the denominator of the historical P&L above.

Cross-exchange. The same market quoted on two venues: what it would cost to buy on the cheaper and sell on the dearer, in the same instant. Both books are read per scan (Binance over WebSocket, MEXC over REST) and they are not simultaneous — the WS snapshot accumulates over up to 4s while the REST reply is one instant — so timing skew, not depth or fees, is the main source of spreads that were never fillable. Treat a reported spread as an upper bound on an upper bound. Turn xchg_enabled off to leave the scan polling funding alone.

India mode. Both legs of a spread dispose of a VDA — USDT included — so both attract TDS. Two legs × 1% TDS ≈ 2% of notional against a spread edge measured in basis points, which is the arithmetic that ended the fill experiment: the withholding is on turnover, not on gains.

Cash-and-carry. Long spot, short the perp, collect funding — price risk cancels between the legs. This board is the observation; the paper positions opened from it are in the panel below, and nothing is traded either way. Net annual % is the annualised rate less the round trip of fees — 2 spot legs at fee_rate plus 2 perp legs at perp_fee_rate, spread over funding_hold_days. The annualised figure assumes the next published rate repeats all year, which it will not; basis, margin and liquidation are not modelled. Every venue is polled on every board, so one symbol can appear more than once and the two full-board venues each contribute the 11 majors plus their own best 20 and worst 5 contracts — a deeply negative rate is a result, not noise.

Paper positions, and still nothing is traded. Long spot, short the perp, at funding_position_size_usdt a leg — recorded here and never booked against the balance above, because a position held for days cannot be reconciled against a P&L whose whole history is atomic round trips. Funding accrues only for settlements the scanner actually observed; a boundary with no rate row inside 24h is skipped rather than estimated, which is why “settled” can lag the position’s age. Nothing is ever opened on an assumed settlement interval.

Predicted % vs realised % is the point. The first is what the rate observed at entry said a year of it would pay; the second is what the position actually paid, net of one round trip of fees, annualised over the days it was really held. Their difference is the “this rate repeats ~1095 times” extrapolation error — measured here rather than asserted. Basis and mark-to-market are not modelled at all, so a realised figure is the funding result, not the trade’s.

Realised % annualises a short hold aggressively. It scales the days actually held up to a year — a position closed after three days is multiplied by about 122, so a few cents of funding either way becomes a double-digit percentage. Read it against “Held” and against “Realised” in USDT, which is the figure that does not move when the hold is short.
